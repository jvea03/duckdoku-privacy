Duckdoku Blast – Privacy Policy

Last updated: 25 November 2026

Duckdoku Blast ("the game") is published by Little Guy Games ("we", "us"). This policy explains what information the game handles and why. The short version: the game keeps your progress on your own device, does not ask you to create an account, and the only information that leaves your device is what Google's advertising and purchase services need to work, anonymous usage statistics that help us improve the game (which you can switch off in Settings), information that helps us measure our advertising (which the same setting controls), and, once the leaderboards are on, your player name, duck picture and anchor scores.

Information stored on your device

Your progress – level reached, coins, boosters, collection, chests, streaks, settings and similar game state – is saved in a file on your device. It is not uploaded to us. Uninstalling the game deletes it. The game does not ask for, or store, your name, email address, contacts, location, photos or any other personal information.

Teams shown in the game are generated on your device; nothing about them is sent to a server. The leaderboards are the one place the game shares something with other players: see "Online leaderboards" below.

Inbox and online leaderboards (Google Firebase)

The game signs your device in anonymously to Google Firebase (Google LLC), the same kind of cloud service as the rest of Google's tools. No account, email address or password is involved: it creates a random identifier for your install and keeps it on your device. Settings shows it as your "Player ID".

The game uses that identifier to check your Inbox for news and gifts we send, either to every player or to your install alone (for example when you contact support about a problem). Only you can read messages sent to your Player ID, and nothing about you is sent to check them.

When the leaderboards are switched on, each time you win a level the game sends that identifier together with your player name, the duck picture you chose and your anchor totals for the day, the week and the season. These appear on the leaderboard, so other players can see your player name, duck and score. Anyone can pick or change a player name in the game; please do not use your real name or any personal information in it.

We do not send your progress, purchases, location, contacts or anything else to the leaderboard. The data is stored on Google's servers in the United States. If you want your leaderboard entries removed, contact us at the support address below with the name you used and we will delete them; uninstalling the game does not by itself remove entries already on the board. You can hide any player from your own boards, or report a name, with the "..." button on their row. The leaderboard can be switched off remotely at any time.

Advertising (Google AdMob)

The game shows rewarded and interstitial ads served by Google AdMob. To serve and measure ads, Google may collect your device's advertising ID, IP address, coarse location derived from it, device and app information, and how you interact with ads. This data is processed by Google under the Google Privacy Policy and AdMob's data practices.

Where required (for example in the EEA and UK), the game shows a consent form before any ad is loaded and respects your choice. You can reset or limit your advertising ID in your device's Google settings, and clearing the game's data (or reinstalling it) shows the consent form again.

If you buy the No Ads Pass, the game stops requesting ads.

Purchases (Apple App Store and Google Play)

In-app purchases are processed by the Apple App Store on iPhone and iPad and by Google Play on Android. We never see or store your payment details. The store provides the game with a purchase record (product and order identifier) so that the items can be granted and restored on this device. Your payment data is covered by Apple's Privacy Policy or the Google Privacy Policy.

Analytics and crash reports

The game sends anonymous usage statistics to GameAnalytics (GameAnalytics Ltd) so we can see which levels are too hard, whether the tutorial is clear and how the in-game economy is balanced. This includes: a random identifier created on your device (it is not your advertising ID and cannot be traced back to you), your device model, operating system and game version, an approximate country derived from your IP address by the service, and gameplay events such as levels started, won or lost, boosters used, ads watched, purchases made and coins earned or spent.

It never includes your name, your advertising ID, anything you type (such as crew chat or your player name), your contacts, your precise location or your payment details. GameAnalytics processes this data under its own privacy policy.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, the game asks for your agreement first and sends no analytics, and stores no analytics ID, unless you say yes. Everywhere else it is on by default. In every region you can change your choice at any time in Settings → Share usage data; turning it off stops the statistics, discards any that were waiting and deletes the random ID. Agreeing is optional and the game plays the same either way. Google Play may also share aggregated install and crash statistics with us through the Play Console; these do not identify you.

Measuring our advertising (Google Analytics for Firebase and Meta)

The game also uses Google Analytics for Firebase (Google LLC), so we can see how many installs come from our advertising, whether those players go on to play (for example, finishing the opening tutorial, coming back the next day, or finishing level 5, 10 or 20), and improve our campaigns. On iPhone and iPad it collects a random identifier for your install of the game, a vendor identifier (an Apple ID shared only by apps from the same developer on your device), your device model, operating system and game version, your approximate location, and app activity such as first opens, sessions, updates, levels finished, ads viewed or tapped, and in-app purchases. On Android (from version 1.0.36) it collects the same kinds of information, with an app instance identifier and your device's advertising ID in place of the Apple identifiers. We may share this information with Google Ads to measure our campaigns. It never includes your name, email address, contacts or anything you type.

On iPhone and iPad the game asks your permission to track (Apple's App Tracking Transparency). From version 1.0.36, Firebase measurement follows the same choice as our usage statistics and does not wait for that answer, but unless you allow tracking it does not read your advertising identifier and your data is not used for ads (Google's ad storage and ad user data consent stay denied). If you allow tracking, Firebase may also use your advertising identifier so Google Ads can measure our campaigns, and Google AdMob may use it to show more relevant ads. You can change your answer at any time in your device's Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking. We have turned off Firebase's use for ad personalization on Android. On both, Firebase follows the same choice as our usage statistics: in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland it stays off unless you agree (the question names Google Analytics for Firebase), and in every region you can turn it off in Settings → Share usage data. Turning it off stops further collection. Google processes this data under the Google Privacy Policy.

From version 1.0.36 the game also uses the Meta (Facebook) SDK (Meta Platforms, Inc.), so we can measure our Facebook and Instagram ads. It receives app installs and opens and the same few milestones (finishing the opening tutorial, coming back the next day and finishing level 10), with your device model, operating system and game version, and your advertising identifier on Android, or on iPhone and iPad only if you allow tracking. It never includes your name, email address, contacts or anything you type, and the game does not use Facebook Login. The Meta SDK follows the same choices as Firebase: it does not start until you have allowed usage statistics, Google's ads consent form (where one is shown) has finished and, on iPhone and iPad, you have allowed tracking; you can turn it off at any time in Settings → Share usage data. Meta processes this data under its own privacy policy.

Children

The game is intended for players aged 13 and over and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has provided personal information through the game, contact us and we will help remove it.

Your choices

  • Reset or limit ad personalisation: device Settings → Google → Ads.
  • Allow or stop usage statistics and advertising measurement: Settings → Share usage data (in the EEA, UK and Switzerland the game asks first).
  • See the consent form again: clear the game's data in device Settings → Apps, or reinstall.
  • Delete all game data: uninstall the game.

Changes to this policy

If the game starts handling data differently – for example if we add online leaderboards or new kinds of data collection – we will update this page and the date at the top before the change ships.
